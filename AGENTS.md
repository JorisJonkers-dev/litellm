## Free groups

Call `free/<model>` (`free/glm-4.7-flash`, `free/gpt-oss-120b`, `free/gemma-4-31b-it`, `free/nemotron-3.5-lightning`) with a key holding the access group `free`. The group holds every zero-cost copy of that model on every free route, and the proxy picks one whose provider is not limited right now; it never reaches a paid deployment. `catalog/sync.py` plans one `free/<model>` group per model, and that group is the only way to reach a free copy:

- **Which copies join.** The free-only routes that record a zero cost (OpenRouter `:free` ids, Groq, Cloudflare, Z.ai's three free ids, Kilo, OVH, Requesty, Naga, Routeway, the second wave), and the free ids of a paid route (OpenCode Go's `-free` ids). `FREE_GROUP_ROUTES` in `sync.py` is the list. Never: the Mistral routes (`vibe*`), Anthropic or any budgeted deployment; The Google free tier joins too (owner, 2026-11-26): the AI Studio key's project has **no billing account**, so the owner pays nothing and is only rate-limited. Every usable Google chat model is free and joins (Gemini, previews and the 4-series included, Gemma); the chat-only rules still apply and a moving `-latest` alias never joins. A model the free tier does not serve answers 429 or 403 and the limiter blocks it; it is never billed. The same principle holds for Poe (no subscription: only its free daily points), Inferbase (no payment method: its daily allowance) and the no-card routes (Cohere trial, SEA-LION, Typhoon, Plugsky, Gonka): no payment method means free and rate limited, so every usable chat model of those routes joins, and a refused request is blocked, never billed. **Adding a billing account or payment method to any of these accounts (Google, Poe, Inferbase or a no-card route) would make this unsafe**: the free groups would then reach billed models, so the route must leave the free groups (and `free_all`) first. NVIDIA NIM (build.nvidia.com) is a free route: its models join once usable, 40 requests a minute per account (unconfirmed). A free copy is reachable only as `free/<model>`: no per-route group, no `opencode-go-free/`, `google-free/` or `public/` group, and the access group `free` alone (`tests/test_free_limits.py` fails on any other path or on `free` in the name after the prefix). Only chat-completion models join: a mode other than chat or a name of an image, embedding, rerank, speech, OCR, parser, music or guard model (`FREE_NON_CHAT`) is refused, and on a `free_all` route or Google a typed listing, models.dev (its own provider, or most providers listing the model) or `free_ids` must show it is chat. Cloudflare's paid-plan models (`paid_only.cloudflare` in `routing.json`, copied from the note on [its pricing page](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers-ai/platform/pricing/): kimi-k2.6, kimi-k2.7-code, glm-5.2, glm-5.3, glm-5.3-flash, deepseek-v4-flash-0731, deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017, deepseek-v4-pro-0813, inkling-2-small) are never free, probed or planned. The model search listing has no documented field for this, so the list is kept by hand. Poe and Inferbase are `free_all` routes: neither account has a payment method (owner, 2026-11-26).
- **How ids are matched.** `free_canonical()`: the canonical name without its date stamp stripped, then without Cloudflare's `@cf/`/`@hf/` prefix and any vendor path (the last path segment is the model), lowercased, without a trailing `:free` or `-free` (or a `free` glued to a date, `deepseek-v4-flash-0731free`), then `aliases` and `free_aliases` in `catalog/routing.json`. `free_aliases` holds only merges models.dev names as one model (`gemma-4-31b` = `gemma-4-31b-it`, `beam` = `beam-1.0-501b-a23b`, `nemotron-3.5-lightning-30b-a3b` = `nemotron-3.5-lightning`) and applies to the free groups only. Never merged: fine-tunes (`gemma-4-26b-a4b-it-darksoul`), safety variants (`gpt-oss-safeguard-20b`), quantised or distilled ids (`-fp8-fast`), and dated snapshots (`deepseek-v4-flash-0731`), unless an alias says so. Router endpoints (`openrouter/free`) are not a model and get no group. `aliases` maps the dated `deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017` (dotted and hyphenated) to `deepseek-v4.1-flash`: DeepSeek's own `deepseek-flash` is that snapshot on models.dev. An audit test (`test_no_free_group_is_a_near_miss_of_another`) fails on two free groups that differ only by a date stamp, punctuation or a variant marker unless they are listed as kept apart: `codestral` and `codestral-2508` (`codestral` is a moving alias, 2024-05 or 25.08 depending on provider), `qwen2.5-coder-32b` and `-instruct` (base model and chat tune), `typhoon-ocr` and `typhoon-ocr-preview` (one provider lists both). `deepseek-v4-flash` and `deepseek-v4-flash-0731` stay apart too (no alias says the April model is the 0731 snapshot), as do `glm-5.3-flash` and `glm-5.3-flashx`. A route serving a model under two ids keeps the shortest.
- **Order.** `free_order` in `routing.json` (lowest first): per-minute providers first (Groq 1, Cloudflare and Z.ai 2, Kilo, OVH, NVIDIA, SEA-LION 3, Electron Hub 4), then the shared daily allowances (UnoRouter, OpenRouter, Poe 5; Requesty, Routeway, Naga, BazaarLink, Plugsky, Airforce 6), then monthly ones (Vercel, OpenCode Go and the per-token hosts 7, Gonka 8, Cohere 9). A route not named comes last.
- **Limits before the call.** `free_limits.<route>` in `routing.json` (table below). `deployment` limits are copied into each deployment's `litellm_params` (`rpm`, `tpm`, `rpd`, `tpd`, `max_parallel_requests`, and `daily_reset_time` HH:MM UTC with a daily limit), and `router_settings.optional_pre_call_checks` holds `enforce_model_rate_limits`, so LiteLLM holds every deployment to them. Verified in the installed 1.109.1 source (`types/router.py` GenericLiteLLMParams, `router_utils/pre_call_checks/model_rate_limit_check.py`) and by `tests/test_free_limits.py` `RouterIntegration` in the image. `shared` budgets (one provider account or IP across all its models), `concurrency` across a route, and everything learned from the providers are kept by `config/free_limits.py`.
- **`config/free_limits.py`** (a `CustomLogger` in `config.yaml`'s `callbacks`, copied into the image). Counts every attempt on a free route just before it goes out (`async_pre_call_check`) per deployment (route + upstream model, so the per-route group, the `free/` group and a tier copy share one count) and per route, adds tokens after a success, and holds requests in flight. It learns from answers: a 429, a 402 or a quota error blocks until `Retry-After`, else `x-ratelimit-reset*`, else the route's next daily reset when the error speaks of a day or quota, else the route's `cooldowns` value (the backstop). A success whose `x-ratelimit-remaining-requests` or `-tokens` is 0 blocks the copy until its `x-ratelimit-reset-*`, before the provider refuses anything (read from the response's `_hidden_params["additional_headers"]`, which 1.109.1 fills with the raw headers prefixed `llm_provider-`, and from the logging payload's `additional_headers`). `scope` says whether a learned limit blocks the deployment or the whole account; `account_match` escalates a matching error to the account; a 402 always blocks the account. `async_filter_deployments` removes every blocked or spent deployment before the router picks, so no request is sent to a limited provider and no retry is spent on one; a deployment is back the moment its block ends or its window turns (minute, clock hour, day from `reset`, calendar month). When none is left the call fails at once with the router's no-deployments error (a tier's next step then serves).
- **No fixed cooldown.** A free deployment has no `cooldown_time`: LiteLLM 1.109.1 puts a deployment's `cooldown_time` before the provider's `Retry-After` (`router_python_core.py`, deployment config > response header > router default), so a fixed value would override what the provider says. Without one a 429 cools for `Retry-After`, else the router default, and `free_limits.py` keeps the copy out for as long as it learned. Inferbase's per-route group keeps its cooldown (budgeted). A group of one free deployment carries `RateLimitErrorAllowedFails: 0` so it still cools.
- **Recorded probe answers.** A negative probe answer (blocked, unknown, error, spent budget) is kept with the route's key fingerprint (a sha256 prefix, never the key, `key_fingerprint` in the provider's metadata). When the key changes, or the answer is older than 7 days (`PROBE_ANSWER_TTL`), the model goes back to what the listing, models.dev and traffic say. Answers from the listing or traffic are decided every run anyway.
- **Restarts.** State is in memory (one `litellm-primary` replica). The first filtered request after a start seeds the counters of the current hour, day and month from the spend log (`LiteLLM_SpendLogs`, hourly sums per `model_id`, bounded to 5 seconds; without a database it logs and counts from zero). A block learned from a 429 is not in the log: after a restart each limited provider answers one 429 again. The store is one class (`MemoryStore`: counters, blocks, slots) so a Redis store can replace it for more replicas.
- **Retries.** `num_retries` stays 2: a known-limited copy is filtered out before the pick and costs no attempt, so the retries are only for a limit nobody knew; a retry with healthy copies left does not sleep. A dead copy (a 401, 403 or 404, or a 400 saying the model is deprecated, gone or missing) is blocked on its own deployment for six hours (`DEAD_BLOCK`, or the reset the answer gives), and `router_settings.retry_policy` gives authentication and bad-request errors their own retries so the next copy is tried.
- **The cost boundary.** `assert_free_groups()` runs at the end of the plan: every deployment in a `free/*` group must carry an explicit zero input and output cost, no budget, the access group `free` only and a free route, or the sync run fails. There is no `default_fallbacks`, and no fallbacks entry is keyed on a `free/*` group, so nothing reachable from one ends at a paid deployment. Every route in a free group is public work only (its provider may train on the prompt); only a key holding `free` reaches a `free/*` group.

## Approving a free id

Routes whose free ids are a static list (`groq`, `cloudflare`, `zai`, `axforge`, `inferbase`, and the second wave's `bazaarlink`, `cohere`, `plugsky`, `poe`, `watsonx`, `together`, `typhoon`, `sealion`, `gonka`, `longcat`, `neosantara`) deploy only ids in `free_ids` of `catalog/routing.json`. The owner can choose only from what the providers list with the real keys, which only the in-cluster sync holds, so the sync scans these routes every run and records every listed id with the signals the listing gives (price fields, free flags, context, modality) as `free_candidates` in the catalogue, marked `approved` (in `free_ids`) or `candidate`. A candidate is never deployed.

- **Free by plan or by price.** `free_by` in `catalog/routing.json` says how a static-list route is free. `plan` (`groq`, `cloudflare`, `zai`, and the no-card accounts `cohere`, `sealion`, `typhoon`, `plugsky`, `gonka`, whose account cannot be charged, so a paid call fails and spends nothing): the plan is free and the provider's listing prices ids per token for paying accounts, so an approved id is deployed whatever the listing shows, and an approved id the listing omits (Z.ai's `*-flash` ids) is still probed. `listed` (`bazaarlink`, `neosantara`): the listing's own price is the signal, free only on an explicit zero input and output price (a missing, empty or non-zero price is not free). `price` (the default, and `together`): the host bills per token, so an approved id the listing prices above zero is refused. An unknown value or a route name that is no route fails the rules load. `free_ids` on a `plan` route must name free-plan models only: nothing backstops the price there, so an id the plan does not cover would bill.
- **Candidates are chat models.** The scan drops embedding, image, speech and rerank models (an approved id always shows). Approved ids are listed first, then the ones the listing suggests.
- **Where to read them.** The "Free-route candidates" section of the models pull request (`bot/model-catalog`, body refreshed on each run) and of the published model overview. Per route: id, state, signals, and `suggested` (the listing gives a zero price or a free flag; no signal means no, and the plan's terms decide).
- **How to approve.** Add the ids to `free_ids.<route>` in `catalog/routing.json` and merge. The next run probes them and plans them.
- **Settings that are not secrets are Vault fields.** `secret/data/platform/litellm` (the UI shows `secret/platform/litellm`) holds them beside the keys, each optional in both `keys:` lists of `deploy/litellm.project.yml` and mapped to its env name by `deploy/env/*/base.env` (`${secret:...#field}`, as the keys are): `cloudflare_account_id` -> `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID`, `bazaarlink_api_base` -> `BAZAARLINK_API_BASE`, `cohere_api_base` -> `COHERE_LIST_BASE` in the catalogue Process only (the sync's list and probe base, the OpenAI-compatible one; LiteLLM's cohere_chat reads `COHERE_API_BASE`, so the proxy gets no such variable and keeps the default base), `watsonx_api_base` -> `WATSONX_API_BASE` (LiteLLM 1.109.1 reads `WATSONX_API_BASE` first, then `WATSONX_URL`; both are set) and `watsonx_project_id` -> `WATSONX_PROJECT_ID`. `ROUTES` entries name any extra env a route `needs`; an empty one skips the route with one route error naming the Vault field (`missing_setting()` in `sync.py`).
- **Cloudflare.** If `CLOUDFLARE_ACCOUNT_ID` (Vault field `cloudflare_account_id`) is empty, the sync asks `GET /client/v4/accounts` with the token and uses the one account it sees (logged; several accounts is an error, set the id). Signals come from `GET /accounts/{id}/ai/models/search`: the item's `properties` (`price`, `context_window`, `beta`). Cloudflare's free tier is an account-wide Neuron allowance, so expect `suggested: no`; the list is a convenience, not a free signal. The search item fields are undocumented in the API reference and parsed defensively; verify against a live run.
- **Failure.** A listing error leaves that route's candidates empty and is reported as a route error; it does not fail the run.

`free_all` lists the routes whose every listed chat model is a free candidate (`groq`, `inferbase`, `cloudflare` (its model list is `/accounts/<id>/ai/models/search`, task "Text Generation" is chat; the Workers Free plan refuses requests past the daily allowance instead of billing), the no-card routes above, and the `listed` routes, where the zero price still decides). `poe` (spends subscription points) and `zai` stay on `free_ids`. A model list that answers 599 failed in transport (DNS, connect, TLS, timeout); the route error now carries the transport error text.

## Second-wave free routes

Sixteen free-only routes beside the first wave (litellm#64, agent-kit#916), reachable only as `free/<model>` with the access group `free` alone, never in `canonical`, zero recorded cost, no budget. Free signals: a `:free` suffix (`electronhub`, `unorouter`), `tier == "free"` (`airforce`), a zero price (`vercel`: `pricing.input` and `pricing.output` both explicitly zero, a number or numeric string, missing or empty is not free; `novita`: the flat `input_token_price_per_m` and `output_token_price_per_m` both zero), and an approved id in `free_ids` for the rest. `unorouter` is anonymous (the first-wave mechanism: no key, no Vault field, no `Authorization` header on list or probe; intended). An approved id on a pay-per-token host (`together`, `typhoon`, `neosantara`) is refused whenever the listing prices it above zero on either side. Only `together` may answer a bare-list `/models`; any other malformed listing is a route error. `together`, `typhoon` and `neosantara` name one approved id each; `bazaarlink`, `cohere`, `plugsky`, `poe`, `watsonx`, `sealion`, `gonka` and `longcat` name none until the owner approves from the candidate scan. Bases not in the spec are LiteLLM 1.109.1's own provider defaults (`poe`, `vercel`, `together`, `neosantara`); `bazaarlink`, `cohere` and `watsonx` have none, so they stay inert until the owner sets one. `cohere` has probe limit 0: its cap is 1,000 calls a month, which an hourly sync cannot probe under half of. `tests/test_routing.py` (`FreeRoutesWave2`) and `tests/test_catalog.py` (`FreeSignals`) cover them.

## Budgets

`accounts` in `catalog/routing.json` is the one source of spending caps: per paid account, the `routes` it bills, `max_budget` and `window` (`{"calendar": "month"}` is `1mo`, `{"days": N}` is `Nd`, an optional `anchor` is the declared cycle start). Owner caps (2026-11-18): OpenCode Go 40 per 30-day cycle (started 2026-11-04 10:41 UTC), Vibe 255, Vibe-study 127, `vibe-api` 25.5 and `vibe-study-api` 12.7 per calendar month (each its own counter; the `-api` windows assumed like their parents); `anthropic` 90 per 30 days and `inferbase` 0.5/day.

- **Mechanism.** `config/account_budget.py` (a `CustomLogger` in `config.yaml`'s `callbacks`, copied into the image) keeps one counter per account. `plan_groups()` writes `model_info.account` on every deployment of an account (`account_budgets()`), the tier copies inherit it, and `config/config.yaml`'s route wildcards carry it by hand. After each success the callback adds `response_cost` to the account; `async_filter_deployments` removes the deployments of a spent account from the pick and raises the router's budget error when none is left. Caps and windows come from `catalog/routing.json` (the image carries it). Windows are the declared ones: `window_start()` in `catalog/sync.py` gives the 1st of the month or the anchored cycle start, so nothing drifts to the first spend (only an unanchored `{"days": N}` window opens at its first spend). Proven in `tests/test_budgets.py`: a Router with the callback blocks the other models, the tier copies and the wildcard of a spent account and spares other accounts, and the real proxy (stub upstreams) did the same.
- **Why not LiteLLM's own counters.** `max_budget` counts per `model_info.id`. Giving every deployment of an account one id would share the counter, but the router keys cooldown and failover by id too: one 429 on one model cooled every model of the account, and a failover inside a group reached the same id twice (reproduced). So each deployment keeps its own id. Tag budgets (`tag_budget_config`) are Enterprise-only and the tag comes from the request. `provider_budget_config` is keyed by the LiteLLM provider, and the four Mistral accounts are all `mistral`.
- **Ids are not model names.** The router resolves a model name equal to a deployment's `model_info.id` to that one deployment and skips every pre-call filter (cooldown, LiteLLM budgets and this callback). A budgeted deployment's id is therefore `<route>:<upstream id>`, never `<route>/<upstream id>`, which a caller can send as a model name (reproduced: `vibe/<id>` walked past a spent cap). `tests/test_budgets.py` fails on a `/` in an account deployment's id.
- **Per-deployment backstop.** Each deployment keeps `max_budget` and `budget_duration` too: LiteLLM's own counter per deployment (its window opens at the first spend). Harmless: it can only stop a single model at the account cap.
- **Persistence.** The counters are in memory, and the first filtered request after a start seeds them from the proxy's spend log (`LiteLLM_SpendLogs`: spend summed per `model_id` since the earliest window start, mapped to accounts through the router's deployments, wildcards included). The query is bounded to 5 seconds; with no database or on any failure the callback logs it, serves, counts in memory and retries after 60 seconds. Spend not yet flushed to the log at restart is lost, and `tests/test_budgets.py` (`Seeding`) covers it with a fake prisma client; the SQL itself was not run against a real Postgres.
- **Sub-caps.** `vibe-api` and `vibe-study-api` are separate accounts. To merge one into another account, list its route there; the sub-cap is then gone.
- **Metrics.** The `model_id` label is `<route>:<upstream id>` for a paid deployment.

## Tier groups

`catalog/routing.json` is the single policy source for the tiers (`tier_groups`); agent-kit renders from it and holds no policy of its own. A tier is a callable model name with an ordered chain:

| Tier | Chain (cost class of each step) |
| --- | --- |
| `tier-free` | free only: Go free models (`free/hy4-flash`, `free/longcat-2.5`, `free/beam-1.0-501b-a23b`), then `free/glm-4.7-flash`, `free/qwen3.8-27b` |
| `tier-vibe` | Vibe Flash first (vibe), then Vibe Mistral Small, then `tier-go-cheap` (go) |
| `tier-go-cheap` | Go Flash models (go) |
| `tier-strong-open` | the opt-in escalation tier: `opencode-go-tier/qwen4-max` (go), then the opt-in open models as budgeted copies: GLM-5.3 (`vibe-tier/zai-glm-5-3`, vibe), Kimi K3.1 Pro (`opencode-go-tier/kimi-k3.1-pro`, go) and GPT-6 Luna (`opencode-go-tier/gpt-6-luna`, go), then `tier-vibe`. Kimi K3.1 Pro has no deployment in the catalogue yet, so it has no copy and the router skips that step until a route serves it |
| `tier-review` | free models from different labs, then Vibe Flash (vibe), then Go Flash (go) |

The ladder is free, then Vibe, then Go. GLM-5.3, Kimi K3.1 Pro and GPT-6 Luna are opt-in only (`opt_in_only`). `tier-strong-open` is itself the opt-in escalation tier (owner, 2026-11-18), so it is the one tier that may hold them: `validate_tiers()` refuses any other chain that holds one, by canonical name, aliases included (a tier that splices `tier-strong-open` in is refused too).

- **Declarative shape.** `tier_groups.<tier>` has `ceiling` (the dearest cost class the tier may hold), `key_models` and `steps`. A step is `{"model", "cost"}` or `{"tier"}`, which splices that tier's steps in place (first occurrence of a model wins, so the ladder cannot loop). The cost class must match the name's prefix (`free/` free, `vibe-tier/` vibe, `opencode-go-tier/` go; a route wildcard prefix is refused, see Budgets), and no step may exceed the ceiling. `tier-free` has ceiling `free`, so it cannot reach a paid deployment; `tier-review` reaches Vibe and Go only through steps that say so.
- **How it is rendered.** `config/config.yaml`'s `router_settings` holds a block between `# BEGIN tiers` and `# END tiers`: `model_group_alias` maps each tier to its first step, and `fallbacks` holds the rest in order. LiteLLM merges an included file's lists but replaces its mappings, so this block cannot live in `models.generated.yaml`. Regenerate it with `python3 catalog/sync.py --render-tiers`; a test fails if the file and `routing.json` disagree. The sync run also validates the tiers and names any free step whose `free/<canonical>` group the plan does not hold yet (the router skips a missing step and the next one serves).
- **Budgets (paid steps).** A paid step is never a route wildcard (the wildcards carry the account too, see Budgets above, but a tier step names a copy). A paid step is `vibe-tier/<upstream id>` or `opencode-go-tier/<upstream id>`, a copy (`tier_copies()`) of the route's own canonical deployment with the **same `model_info.id`** and `model_info.account`, budget and cooldown. A tier call and a direct call draw on one account counter (tested both ways). `assert_tier_copies()` fails the sync unless every copy shares an id with a deployment of its route and carries the route's budget from `routing.json`; a step the catalogue has no deployment for gets no copy, is unknown to the router and is skipped, never served by a wildcard. The cap is one counter per account (see Budgets above), shared with the route's other deployments. `tests/test_tiers.py` runs each budget scenario in its own process, because LiteLLM keeps budget state process-wide and only the first Router built enforces it.
- **No `default_fallbacks`,** and no fallbacks entry keyed on a `free/*` group.
- **Keys.** A key reaches a model name or access group it holds, and a tier name is neither (it is an alias), so a key lists the tier name beside what the chain needs. `enforce_fallback_model_access` is on in this LiteLLM, so a step the key does not hold is skipped: the key's list is what stops a chain, and it is the only thing that does. The lists, kept in `key_models` and tested against each chain:
  - the existing `free` key: `["free", "tier-free"]` (it already holds `free`; add `tier-free`);
  - `tier-vibe`: `["tier-vibe", "vibe-tier", "opencode-go-tier"]` (it ends in Go);
  - `tier-go-cheap`: `["tier-go-cheap", "opencode-go-tier"]`;
  - `tier-strong-open`: `["tier-strong-open", "vibe-tier", "opencode-go-tier", "strong-open-tier"]` (an escalation key, not a workstation key). The opt-in copies (`vibe-tier/zai-glm-5-3`, `opencode-go-tier/kimi-k3.1-pro`, `opencode-go-tier/gpt-6-luna`) are in the access group `strong-open-tier` alone, not in `vibe-tier` or `opencode-go-tier`, so no other tier key can call them by name; `validate_tiers()` refuses `strong-open-tier` in any other tier's `key_models` and requires it in this one's, and no tier falls back into `tier-strong-open`;
  - `tier-review`: `["free", "tier-review", "vibe-tier", "opencode-go-tier"]`.
  `vibe-tier` and `opencode-go-tier` are access groups of the budgeted copies. No tier key holds `vibe/*` or `opencode-go/*`, so a tier key cannot reach the unbudgeted wildcards.

`tests/test_tiers.py` loads the rendered block into LiteLLM's Router (mocked upstream, run in the image), walks every chain with each step rate-limited in turn, and proves that a spent Go budget stops `tier-go-cheap`. `tests/image.test.mjs` boots the real image with this config and calls the tiers through the proxy.

## Probing

A chat request spends a free tier's daily quota, so the sync sends none by itself. It decides from the provider's listing, the public catalogue models.dev (`reference` in `catalog/routing.json` maps a route to its provider id there, with the measured coverage) and real traffic; see "Listing, public catalogue and traffic" in README.md. `python3 catalog/sync.py --probe <route/model>` sends one request for debugging. To feed traffic evidence, set `TRAFFIC_PROMETHEUS_URL` in the catalogue Process to the in-cluster Prometheus (the Process then needs egress to it); unset, the sync runs on the listing and models.dev alone.
